[title]
Clarity is a module for VCV Rack, from Dreamer Development.
Designed to bring visual and interaction clarity to your rack.
On the right is the Clarity module itself, and beside it Test Gear, another module coming from Dreamer Development.
Here are the features of Clarity.

[a-rack-running-clarity]
A rack running Clarity.
Every jack is coloured for what it carries, and every knob is drawn the same way whichever plugin made it.
The cables are coloured for where they go, with dashes drifting slowly along them towards their destination.
The scopes clipped to terminals are widgets from Test Gear, another module coming from Dreamer Development.

[colour-code-jacks]
Colour code jacks.
Every jack in the rack is given a coloured ring for the kind of signal it carries: [[slnc 200]] yellow for audio, orange for control voltage, blue for gates and triggers, and green for pitch.
The same ring says which way the signal goes. [[slnc 200]] It hugs the outer edge of an output, and the hole of an input.
Colour tells you what it carries, and shape tells you which way it goes.

[colour-code-cables]
Colour code cables.
Cables take the same four colours, and a cable takes the colour of the port it arrives at.
Move it somewhere else and it takes the colour of its new destination.
So the colour tells you what a cable is being used for, which depends on where it goes rather than on how the signal was made.

[animate-cable-directions]
Animate cable directions.
Dashes drift slowly along every cable, from its source towards its destination.
The length of the dashes depends on what the cable carries, so one glance tells you which way the signal is flowing and roughly what kind of signal it is.

[consistent-knob-style]
Consistent knob style.
Every knob in the rack is drawn with the same face, whichever plugin it came from.
Notice how much more consistent the rack looks with one knob style across all of it.
And like every other feature of Clarity, you can switch this off if you prefer the original knobs.

[pinch-to-zoom]
Pinch TOO zoom.
Pinch on a trackpad and the rack grows between your fingers.
The view scales smoothly while you pinch, and everything is redrawn sharply the moment you let go.

[cable-trace-assist]
Cable trace assist.
Hover either end of a cable and a handle appears on it.
Click the handle and every other cable is hidden, so you can follow one lead through a tangle.
Click on any module panel to bring the others back.

[add-and-move-cables-without-dragging]
Add and move cables without dragging.
You do not have to hold the button down. [[slnc 250]] To start a cable, click an empty terminal, move to where you want it, and click again to connect it.
To move a cable that is already patched, click it to pick it up, carry it to another terminal, and click to connect it there. [[slnc 250]] Dragging still works exactly as it always did, so whichever way you do it is right.

[several-cables-on-one-jack]
Several cables on one jack.
A click picks up the cable on top. [[slnc 250]] Click the same jack again, without moving off it, and you are holding the one below it.
Click a third time and you have a new cable instead, a third one from the same jack. [[slnc 250]] Both of the cables that were there are still connected.

[test-gear-preview]
A preview of Test Gear.
Test Gear brings thirteen widgets that clip onto any terminal in the rack: [[slnc 200]] to see a signal, to hear it, to inject one, or to change what is already there.
Here three oscilloscopes are clipped straight onto the patch, each one capturing at the engine's own sample rate rather than the frame rate.
